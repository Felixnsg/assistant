I'm excited to apply for the RISE Mentor position. Working with pre-K children to build their literacy skills and confidence before they start their academic journey sounds incredibly meaningful. As someone who learned English as a third language, I understand how crucial those early years are for language development.

My experience at Bellevue College taught me how to connect with students from all backgrounds. I worked with many students whose families didn't speak English at home. Being able to communicate in French, Swahili, and several African dialects helped me build trust not just with students, but with their families too. I learned that when parents feel included, children thrive.

What really excites me about RISE is the focus on both literacy and social-emotional learning. Four and five-year-olds aren't just learning their letters. They're learning how to be students, how to work with others, how to express themselves. I've always been good at getting on kids' level. High energy and noise don't bother me. Actually, that's when learning feels most alive. When kids are excited, moving around, fully engaged.

I've spent time creating educational materials and activities for young learners. Breaking down complex ideas into simple, fun activities. Using stories, games, and hands-on learning to keep kids interested. I know that reading to children isn't just about saying words on a page. It's about bringing stories to life, using different voices, getting kids to predict what happens next. Making them love books before they even know how to read them.

The team aspect of RISE really appeals to me. Working with the same classroom all year, collaborating with other mentors, planning together. That consistency matters for young children. They need to know who's showing up for them. And working as a team means we can learn from each other, share what works, and create better experiences for the kids.

Being part of that foundation for Central Seattle pre-K students would be an honor.
