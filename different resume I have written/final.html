Problem 1)

a) Testing whether Asian elephants in captivity have shorter lifespans.

H₀: μ = 28 years
H₁: μ < 28 years

Given: n = 16, x̄ = 19.5, s = 2.84

Test statistic:

t = (x̄ - μ₀)/(s/√n)

t = (19.5 - 28)/(2.84/√16)

t = -8.5/(2.84/4)

t = -8.5/0.71

t = -11.97

b) At α = 0.01 significance level:

df = 15, critical value = -2.602

Since -11.97 < -2.602, we reject H₀.

Conclusion: There is strong evidence that captive elephants have shorter lifespans.

n = 28, df = 27, t = 2.052

Mean = (1.40 + 1.57)/2 = 1.485 years

Margin of error = (1.57 - 1.40)/2 = 0.085

0.085 = 2.052(s/√28)

s = 0.085(5.292)/2.052 = 0.219 years

Mean = 1.485 years, SD = 0.219 years

b) Why the representative is wrong:

The confidence interval is about the population mean, not individual bulbs. It tells us we're 95% confident the true average lifetime is between 1.40 and 1.57 years. It does not mean that only 2.5% of bulbs have lifetimes below 1.40 years. Individual bulbs could have lifetimes well outside this range. The CI is for the mean, not for individual values.
